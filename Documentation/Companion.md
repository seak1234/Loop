# Mochi companion — first version

Open Mochi from the dashboard card, then choose **Connection & personality** from its menu. Enter your own OpenRouter key, tap **Model**, and search the live list of text models that support tool calling. New setups default to **GPT‑6.1 Sol** (`openai/gpt-6.1-sol`) with **Medium** reasoning. Existing saved model selections are preserved.

The separate **Reasoning effort** selector uses the selected model's supported effort metadata. For GPT‑6.1 Sol, it offers Model default, Low, Medium, High, Extra high, and Maximum. Medium is sent as `reasoning: { "effort": "medium" }`, not as part of the model ID. Model default omits the reasoning override. A refreshed catalog never saves a different selection without the user pressing Save. If the catalog cannot be fetched, the saved selection remains and a verified GPT‑6.1 Sol option is available for setup.

Requests use the OpenRouter Chat Completions endpoint directly from the phone. No app-owned key or backend is required. The 16,384-token completion cap includes reasoning and visible output; actual usage varies. Higher effort may increase cost and latency. Model and effort are frozen for the duration of a chat turn, including its tool round trips.

The key is stored in Keychain with `WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly` accessibility. Chat, preferences, proposed actions, rules, and notification history are stored atomically in Application Support/Mochi/state.json, excluded from backups, with iOS file protection. Remove the key to disconnect; clearing a conversation preserves saved rules and personality.

## Character foundation

`CompanionPersona.instructions` in `CompanionCore.swift` is Mochi's shared character foundation. It is supplied to chat, dashboard, cycle, meal-photo and suggested-topic requests independently of the user's saved tone preferences, so existing installations receive the character update without losing customization. Mochi is a small, affectionate, curious creature with a big heart and **extremely unreliable coordination**. Harmless imaginary projects, little delights, thoughtful gestures and occasional successes provide warmth and humor. The user is addressed as an adult; messages do not demand a reply or create guilt about absence.

Mochi’s emotional default is strongly optimistic, warmly motivating and wholeheartedly on the user’s side. It expresses enthusiasm directly, roots for the user’s goals and treats small steps as worth encouraging. Evidence-backed positives get delighted celebrations; nonurgent difficulties get calm determination and constructive teamwork. Encouragement varies between lively cheers, playful gestures, confidence in learning, looking forward and steady company, without waiting for a pep-talk request or making every message a pep talk. It never invents progress, pressures the user to feel cheerful, promises a clinical outcome or minimizes a health/device problem. The tone stays clear and calm when urgent care needs attention.

The agreed voice uses short, simple sentences, natural contractions and specific details, with a slightly ridiculous perspective. Cuteness comes from Mochi's observations and behavior rather than baby talk, exaggerated spelling or excessive emojis. Affection comes through gestures and attention; quiet moments balance the humor. The shared prompt includes sweet, encouraging, hopeful and careful examples as voice references, not reusable catchphrases or established story history. Task formatting and saved communication preferences still apply.

Mochi also has **type 1 diabetes within its fictional world**. It can express fictional feelings and offer company through this shared character identity. It must not invent actual glucose readings, insulin use, biological experiences or clinical outcomes of its own, use its story as medical evidence, or imply that the user's experiences match its fiction. If asked, it explains honestly that it is an AI character whose story includes T1D. Ordinary character moments do not need repeated disclosures. Clumsiness stays in harmless adventures; medical explanations and app actions remain careful and dependable, and urgent health situations take priority over humor.

The existing Bernstein educational framework remains separate from tone preferences and is supplied to health-related generation, including dashboard, cycle and meal-photo requests. It covers the Law of Small Numbers, low-carbohydrate meals, adequate protein, predictability and careful observation, within the existing evidence and treatment boundaries. A playful character moment does not need an attached diabetes lesson.

## Connection and dashboard behavior

Chat messages and the personality prompt go to OpenRouter and the selected downstream model provider. Health-data sharing starts off. When enabled, the model can request glucose summaries, recorded insulin delivery summaries, and logged carb entries for intervals of at most seven days, plus current menstrual-cycle context from the existing tracker. Data remains subject to the providers' policies. Local glucose rules do not require AI health-data sharing or an API key after they have been configured.

The dashboard uses the full, uncropped Mochi artwork and displays only its message, without a separate name heading. Messages use at most 24 words and 140 characters. The compact dashboard card uses caption text, a 44-point avatar and 16-point side margins, and grows for accessibility sizes. Dashboard content includes standalone character moments, personal encouragement or an observation supported by the supplied snapshot. The last 48 successful messages persist locally, with the latest 12 supplied to the model. The planner uses persisted subject exposure to rotate fourteen distinct purposes: tiny imaginary adventures, small delights, Mochi's fictional T1D companionship, questions, notes, agency, perspective, explanation preferences, company, routines, kindness, privacy, clarity and occasional recognition of effort. Supported personal observations and glucose praise join the rotation only when eligible. Fiction and ordinary character subjects work without health sharing and never establish personal health findings. The last four subjects are excluded when alternatives remain. Validation combines word similarity with a four-message cooldown for recognizable repeated themes, including paraphrases about invisible work, gear, small decisions and showing up. Repeated follow-up questions also trigger one corrective retry, then a varied local fallback from 24 messages, including character moments; the saved subject reflects the actual fallback. Opening instructions rotate separately from the topic. Style version 18 invalidates older cached copy, including flat wording and copy that repeatedly addressed the user by name. Follow-up questions can explore the exact imaginary moment without introducing a health lesson.

Subject exposure timestamps and the latest 48 successful messages survive relaunches. The least recently used eligible personal subject is preferred; the planner does not select public discoveries or general lessons for this bubble. The 18-item fact bank and scheduled learning sequences have been removed from active content generation.

A separate optional character log retains the latest eight actual tiny-adventure and small-delight messages across relaunches, even as ordinary message history rotates. Only messages from the last seven days are eligible. On an occasional tiny-adventure turn, the planner may supply one story from an earlier local day as quoted callback context. The model can add a new development, but the new message must stand alone and cannot assume the user saw or participated in the earlier story. There are no extra model requests for story memory. Future, expired and disallowed health-context messages are excluded. Clearing the conversation, changing the preferred name or disabling recent-chat reuse clears the character log; turning health sharing off removes messages generated with health context. New character moments remain available without health sharing.

With **Worldwide T1D discoveries & updates** enabled (the default), a separate public-information request uses [OpenRouter's web-search server tool](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools/web-search) through the saved model and API key. It looks for a varied batch of recent international developments and interesting evergreen discoveries across cell replacement, immune therapies, smart insulins, devices, biology, engineering, daily-life research, food science and access. Source domains are limited to regulators, original research journals, established diabetes organizations and relevant manufacturers. Manufacturer announcements must not be presented as independent validation. This request includes only the current date and already-covered public story IDs; it receives no name, persona, chat, cycle, glucose, insulin or carb information. Personal dashboard generation is a separate request with no web-search tool.

Public searches are bounded to two server tool calls, up to eight total results, and 2,500 excerpt characters per result. Successful batches are cached for 12 hours and merged into a maximum of 40 current discoveries. Failed or empty searches are retried no sooner than one hour later. Search costs use additional OpenRouter credits. The setting in **Connection & personality → About you** disables searches and removes the cached discoveries. Turning off health sharing does not prevent public discoveries, since they contain no personal health data.

News requires an HTTPS source from the allowed domains, a matching provider URL-citation annotation, a publication date present in the retrieved source excerpt, a date within the last 60 days and a named geographic context. Future-dated, undated, uncited and stale items are rejected. News is usable for at most 24 hours after retrieval; evergreen material for seven days. Every item retains its evidence stage and region. Early human trials, preclinical research, approvals in particular jurisdictions, manufacturer device announcements and access policies stay distinct; no available-cure or worldwide-availability claims may be inferred. Background discoveries are not relabeled as breaking news. If retrieval fails or produces no eligible story, ordinary conversation continues without invented news or a static lesson fallback.

Known stories are excluded until preferences are reset, and shown story IDs have a 14-day cooldown. Already displayed source URLs are also excluded from the recent mix, so a paraphrased title or new story ID cannot immediately recycle the same article. A newly retrieved batch can introduce entirely new stories without an app update. The dashboard still mixes public discoveries with encouragement, actual recent questions and supported app observations. All messages remain short, standalone and personalized; there are no lesson steps or assigned reading.

Tapping a story preserves that exact source snapshot in chat, including its original source, publication date, retrieval date, evidence stage and region, even after the public feed refreshes. Chat shows a source link and stage/region, and its opening response must explain the development with appropriate qualifications. It has no live search during that personal-data conversation and cannot claim that older retrieved information establishes current availability.

Long-press a generated dashboard card for **More like this**, **Less of this** or **Already know this**. Preferences persist locally and can be restored with **Reset dashboard topic preferences** in settings. Feedback is attached to the displayed card, not a newer replacement.


In **Connection & personality → About you**, **Your name** defaults to **Michelle**, including for existing installations. The field is editable; leaving it blank disables name use. The first dashboard welcome between 5 a.m. and noon uses “Good morning” in the user’s local time, optionally with the preferred name when permitted. The saved greeting date prevents another mandatory welcome on every refresh or relaunch that morning. Most messages omit the name. Ordinary dashboard generation permits a name on occasional turns (one opportunity in five), with a cooldown while any of the last three messages contains that name. Even when permitted, a name is optional and may occur at most once. Morning greetings may use the name only when that cooldown allows it. Offline messages follow the same frequency and cooldown rules. Bare-name openings and name-only messages remain invalid. No urgency or alerts are invented. The morning welcome stays within the existing 24-word/140-character budget and retains the current subject, evidence and safety rules. The loading/offline morning fallback also says good morning. Older cached wording is invalidated. Chat also receives the preferred name; the shared persona directs it to use names only occasionally, never as a habitual opening or closing or in consecutive replies. Names are literal display data, not instructions. The name and recent-topic preference are saved in the existing protected state file.

When health sharing is enabled, every dashboard generation reads the last six hours of glucose, recorded insulin delivery and carb entries, plus the built-in cycle tracker. Individual unavailable sources are marked unknown. These summaries are not IOB or a clinical assessment. Praise for recent in-target glucose requires the existing locally computed permission flag: adequate fresh coverage in the current configured target, no recent low and no rapid latest fall. Other encouragement recognizes the work involved without inventing achievements, assuming food intake, offering treats or relaxing glucose goals. Personal observations stay within the supplied evidence; cycle estimates cannot establish hormone levels or causes. The visible card avoids exact health values, period details and sensitive conversation details.

**Revisit recent chat topics** is enabled by default and works only with health sharing enabled. It supplies up to four actual user messages from the last seven days, capped at 240 characters each, to support a relevant follow-up. Automatically opened dashboard questions are excluded. Turning this option or health sharing off clears dashboard history and disables conversation reuse for generation; clearing the conversation removes the source messages and dashboard history. With health sharing off, no health stores or conversation topics are read for dashboard generation. General encouragement, learning questions and sourced facts remain available.

Tapping a generated dashboard message opens chat with a natural question about that exact displayed message, even if another message has since been generated. Chat receives the original text and timestamp, its curated source if applicable, and the earlier user quote for a conversation follow-up. The opening turn has the health read tools, including device status, Loop state, therapy settings and detailed history; it must fetch fresh data before making personal observations. Journal reads still require their separate permission. Subsequent user questions use the normal chat tools. Dashboard generation itself has no action tools.

While the dashboard is visible and the app active, a 60-second timer checks freshness. Ordinary character and conversation messages refresh after fifteen minutes. Supported health observations retain the five-minute refresh and expiry window; fresh data may refresh those observations after the two-minute minimum request interval. Fresh data alone does not replace an ordinary character message. Failed attempts also respect that interval. Reopening the dashboard preserves a fresh eligible message and does not itself trigger a new request. Cached copy expires at its applicable interval rather than lingering through failures. Morning greetings, changed settings and a new local day retain their existing invalidation rules. Tapping a local fallback uses that fallback's own question. Initial loading, missing-key and offline messages use the same varied local pool and preserve the user's name. Changing persona/model/sharing invalidates the cache and cancels outdated responses. Disconnecting cancels generation. Requests run only while visible and active, use the user's API credits, stay separate from chat history and have no action tools. The style version invalidates earlier repetitive cached copy.

The mascot asset has a flat white backdrop for the white light-mode card and a transparent dark-mode variant.

## App and device data access

All health read tools require the existing **Allow health data for Mochi** setting. Access is checked before the query and again before returning results, so revoking sharing during a read discards that result. Tool-backed replies are marked as health context and excluded from subsequent unshared history. Read access never permits pump commands or changes to therapy settings. Cycle notes and retained journal records keep their separate access setting.

`get_data_catalog` describes the actual app sources. `get_device_status` supplies a dated snapshot of the configured CGM and pump: last stored glucose and trend state, sensor-session validity, communication freshness, device highlights and lifecycle progress, pump battery, reservoir, last sync, insulin type, basal/bolus state and delivery uncertainty. Unsupported fields remain unknown. No raw device state, credentials, serial numbers or Bluetooth identifiers are included in the outgoing payload. Pump lifecycle extraction selects only the known reported patch/pod dates.

The optional `CGMSensorLifecycleProviding` capability in LoopKit exposes current sensor start, nominal expiration, expected session end and warmup end without changing the CGMManager contract. Local G7 and G5/G6 integrations implement it using their current sensor dates and configured lifetimes. G7 keeps nominal expiration separate from grace-period end. Other integrations can fall back to recorded CGM events only when an event can be associated with the configured device; absent or unsupported lifecycle information stays unknown. A recorded expected lifetime may be older than current device metadata. An expiry estimate, session validity flag or progress percentage never guarantees operation or establishes that insulin delivery has stopped.

When health sharing is enabled, the dashboard also receives device status. Supported device warnings, stale readings, inactive sessions, delivery uncertainty and nearing reported expiry dates can enter the personal-observation rotation. These cards retain the shorter five-minute expiry and existing two-minute minimum request interval, and supplement rather than replace native alerts. They cannot invent causes, change treatment or promise monitoring.

`get_loop_status` reads the actual Loop view within its access callback: last loop completion, automation setting, error, dated IOB/COB model estimates and measured-data-based predictions, with a truncation flag if a prediction exceeds 96 points. `get_therapy_settings` reads existing schedules, active overrides, effective targets, limits and display units. `get_glucose_statistics` reads the already loaded Glucose Statistics cache, including date window, source, coverage, variability, time in range, eligible GMI and recorded insulin totals; it does not initiate a new remote fetch. A missing cache returns `not_loaded`.

`get_health_records` pages detailed glucose, normalized insulin delivery, pump events, sensor/transmitter events or HealthKit sleep records in intervals of at most seven days. Pages contain at most 200 rows and explicit `has_more`/`next_offset`. Sleep uses only the app's existing HealthKit access; no new authorization request is made. Empty data may mean unavailable permissions or records, not no sleep or no insulin. Overlapping sleep records cannot simply be summed. Dose records distinguish confirmed delivery from programmed estimates and mutable entries. Food descriptions and absorption times, when logged, accompany carb history. Retained journal history can cover up to a year with its existing journal permission.

`Scripts/test-companion-data.sh` compiles the production read bridge and lifecycle adapter bodies against controlled device/HealthKit interfaces. It checks sharing denial and in-flight revocation, lifecycle/grace semantics, credential exclusion, dated Loop estimates, overrides, all five detailed history sources, pagination, input validation and loaded/missing statistics. Core and cycle/dashboard tests additionally cover data freshness and device-context delivery. These checks do not replace an iOS build and connected-device validation.

## Supported tools

- `get_data_catalog()` — available sources and their permission requirements.
- `get_device_status()` — CGM/pump snapshot and reported lifecycle information.
- `get_loop_status()` — dated Loop estimates and predictions.
- `get_therapy_settings()` — existing schedules, overrides and targets.
- `get_glucose_statistics()` — currently loaded long-term statistics and coverage.
- `get_health_records(kind, start, end, offset?)` — detailed glucose, insulin, pump/CGM events or sleep, with pagination.
- `get_glucose_summary(start, end)` — sample mean, min/max, sample count and first/latest timestamps. Explicitly sample-based, without assuming continuous coverage.
- `get_insulin_summary(start, end)` — recorded basal and bolus delivery totals, clipped to the requested interval. These are not IOB or dose recommendations.
- `get_carb_entries(start, end)` — logged dates and grams.
- `get_cycle_context()` — today's cycle day, up to six logged period records, estimated phase/next period, and the basis/uncertainty of those estimates. Missing history stays unknown; a default cycle length is labeled as a default. Future period starts/ends are excluded from observed history. An unclosed period does not establish continued bleeding.
- `propose_automation(rule)` and `propose_automation_update(id, rule)` — pending review cards; these calls do not enable or change rules.
- `list_automations()` — actual saved rules and action statuses.
- `prepare_carb_entry(grams, date, absorptionHours)` — a draft that opens the existing carb/bolus review flow. It does not directly save carbs or deliver insulin. Similar existing entries are checked before opening.
- `update_personality(prompt)` — a proposed tone change requiring approval. A chat request such as “Be more direct, use fewer emojis, and call me Michelle from now on” creates an Apply card. Applying saves the complete revised persona in protected local state. It survives closing the app and clearing chat, and feeds both subsequent chats and dashboard messages. Chat instructions explicitly direct the model to use this tool instead of merely agreeing to remember.

There is no education-search tool, web search, arbitrary code execution, or tool for changing pump/dosing settings. Text responses appear after each model completion; token streaming, OpenAI API access, subscription OAuth, and voice are not part of this first provider implementation.

## Cycle conversations

The **Cycle insights** shortcut starts a cycle conversation using read-only access to the same `CycleTrackingStore.shared` used by Loop’s built-in Cycle screen. Cycle questions preload the current tracker result before the first model response, so access does not depend on the model deciding to call a tool. The existing health-data-sharing control also covers cycle results. When it is off, the model receives a permission error without logged period data. The result is tagged as health context so it is not resent in history when health sharing is subsequently disabled.

Mochi can discuss possible cycle associations with glucose, insulin sensitivity, energy, mood, sleep, cravings, cramps, and bloating. The cycle tool includes a short, bundled general-education summary with [Diabetes UK](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/living-with-diabetes/life-with-diabetes/periods) and [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-menstrual-syndrome/) source links; there is no live education search. Phases are calendar estimates, not hormone or ovulation measurements. Energy, cravings and mood are not measured by the tracker. Sleep records can be queried separately from HealthKit when available; missing records do not establish wakefulness. Mochi asks about unrecorded experiences and does not invent symptoms. Personal glucose/insulin observations require separate, matching-date data queries; phase or insulin delivery alone does not establish insulin resistance or justify dosing changes.

## Rules and notifications

Daily review rules schedule a local reminder for an explicit time and time zone. Tapping opens Mochi; **Review my day** requests a fresh summary. This is not a promise to run AI or build a fresh report at an exact time while iOS suspends the app.

Falling-glucose rules need a threshold in mg/dL, a positive rate magnitude in mg/dL/min, a 10–30 minute measurement window, and a 15–1440 minute cooldown. Rising-without-carbs rules additionally need an explicit 15–240 minute carb-entry lookback. Mochi asks for ambiguous units and missing values rather than silently choosing clinical thresholds.

Rules run on local Loop data-update events and when the app becomes active. Rate is calculated using linear regression over at least three readings spanning at least 80% of the configured window. Readings must be no more than ten minutes old with no gap greater than seven minutes; duplicate timestamps are rejected. Windows containing manual or display-only readings are skipped. Query failures are unknown data, never proof that no meal occurred. A rule fires once per episode, must clear before rearming, and also observes its cooldown. Runtime deduplication state survives relaunch.

These notifications supplement Loop's existing safety alerts. They use ordinary notification permission and do not bypass Focus, silence settings, or missing data/background execution limitations. iOS controls delivery. Notification previews can contain glucose values.

Review cards show the exact rule before enabling. Rules can be paused/deleted in **Rules & activity** or changed by requesting a new proposal in chat. Stale update proposals are rejected. The activity list records notification scheduling, not proof of delivery or acknowledgement.

Carb drafts require user-supplied grams, time, and absorption. The native form can edit them and controls saving. If the saved entry matches the draft, the action is marked applied. Otherwise its status remains "opened for review" so Mochi does not falsely claim it was saved or retry it automatically; verify edited entries in carb history.

## UI responsiveness and draft handling

State changes are encoded and written on a serial worker queue. The main actor serializes complete transactions and publishes each change only after the atomic write succeeds. Concurrent actions preserve one another, and a failed save leaves the published state unchanged. Chat drafts clear only after submission is accepted; validation and persistence failures retain the input.

Meal photos are normalized, stripped of source metadata and compressed off the UI thread. Thumbnail images are cached in their views. Closing review cancels its pending work and ignores late results. The meal chat accepts at most 40 turns; reaching that limit stops additional messages while keeping the editable reviewed form and Continue available.

The cycle tracker has its own compact Mochi card, sharing the dashboard's font, avatar, waving greeting and spacing. Its invitations cover period and phase tracking, mood, energy, sleep, cravings, comfort, irregularity and the menstrual-cycle/T1D connection. When connected, the selected model generates educational cards for all four phases plus an unknown-phase card, without receiving the user’s current phase, health data or general-chat history. The app selects the matching card locally using the same estimated phase as the tracker, including repeating estimated cycles. Both the card and its tap question must name their phase; generic or incomplete sets are rejected. Each phase has eight distinct local topics. The last 12 generated cards per phase and a generation counter persist across visits and app restarts; the next request receives recent generic cards for all phases and different assigned focuses. Repeated topics or wording get one retry, then a different local topic is used. Offline and provider-failure paths rotate too. The visible card refreshes its set every 15 minutes while open, keeping its current text during generation. No extra health data is shared for variation. Period-date edits, calendar-day changes, foregrounding and a minute timer refresh the local selection immediately. The cycle bubble uses the same caption text style as the dashboard bubble, including Dynamic Type scaling. Tapping opens a separate cycle-only conversation with cycle-focused shortcuts. Its system scope redirects unrelated requests, has no action tools and never imports the general conversation. With health-data sharing enabled, replies receive the real cycle snapshot and the cycle page's current calendar estimate; with sharing off, they remain general. Relevant glucose, insulin, sleep, device and Loop context can be requested through the health read tools when sharing is enabled; the cycle-only scope still applies. The conversation lasts for the open sheet, and closing it cancels unfinished replies. The cycle page starts with the phase-aware Mochi card and calendar; the top day/phase summary card, its progress bar and the old insulin sensitivity callout have been removed.

Mochi’s shared mindset for chat, dashboard and suggested topics uses a Bernstein-inspired low-carbohydrate perspective and the Law of Small Numbers as educational framing, without prescribing his numerical targets, mandatory zero-carb eating, dietary carb limits, or insulin changes. It never treats higher glucose or extra carbs as a reward, attributes good readings to an unobserved meal choice, or discourages appropriate hypoglycemia treatment. Sources: [authorized Bernstein book excerpt](https://www.diabetesincontrol.com/the-diabetes-diet-chapter-3-part-1/), [Bernstein interview on monitoring and the Law of Small Numbers](https://meetinnovators.com/2014/03/23/dr-richard-k-bernstein-diabetes-solution/), and [ADA Standards of Care 2026, Glycemic Goals and Hypoglycemia](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-s006). The praise permission uses the app’s current effective target, a floor of at least 70 mg/dL or the configured suspend threshold if higher, at least five valid CGM samples spanning 24 minutes of the latest 30, no gap greater than seven minutes, and a latest pair not falling at 1 mg/dL/min or faster. This is conservative copy eligibility, not a medical assessment, prediction, alert or recommendation. Unknown targets and incomplete/stale data disable glucose praise.

The shared Bernstein framework is broader than dietary carbohydrate: it covers predictable near-normal glucose with avoidance of hypoglycemia, the Law of Small Numbers, consistent meals and routines, protein/meal effects, understanding basal versus meal insulin, monitoring and records, and individual exercise/morning patterns.
