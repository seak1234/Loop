# Mochi companion — first version

Open Mochi from the dashboard card, then choose **Connection & personality** from its menu. Enter your own OpenRouter key, tap **Model**, and search the live list of text models that support tool calling. New setups default to **GPT‑6.1 Sol** (`openai/gpt-6.1-sol`) with **Medium** reasoning. Existing saved model selections are preserved.

The separate **Reasoning effort** selector uses the selected model's supported effort metadata. For GPT‑6.1 Sol, it offers Model default, Low, Medium, High, Extra high, and Maximum. Medium is sent as `reasoning: { "effort": "medium" }`, not as part of the model ID. Model default omits the reasoning override. A refreshed catalog never saves a different selection without the user pressing Save. If the catalog cannot be fetched, the saved selection remains and a verified GPT‑6.1 Sol option is available for setup.

Requests use the OpenRouter Chat Completions endpoint directly from the phone. No app-owned key or backend is required. The 16,384-token completion cap includes reasoning and visible output; actual usage varies. Higher effort may increase cost and latency. Model and effort are frozen for the duration of a chat turn, including its tool round trips.

The key is stored in Keychain with `WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly` accessibility. Chat, preferences, proposed actions, rules, and notification history are stored atomically in Application Support/Mochi/state.json, excluded from backups, with iOS file protection. Remove the key to disconnect; clearing a conversation preserves saved rules and personality.

Chat messages and the personality prompt go to OpenRouter and the selected downstream model provider. Health-data sharing starts off. When enabled, the model can request glucose summaries, recorded insulin delivery summaries, and logged carb entries for intervals of at most seven days, plus current menstrual-cycle context from the existing tracker. Data remains subject to the providers' policies. Local glucose rules do not require AI health-data sharing or an API key after they have been configured.

The dashboard uses the full, uncropped Mochi artwork and displays only its message, without a separate name heading. Messages use at most 24 words and 140 characters; the compact card grows to show the full text, including at larger accessibility sizes. A local planner chooses a subject, and the selected OpenRouter model writes a fitting encouragement, supported observation, curiosity question, sourced fact or follow-up to an actual recent user question. It considers relevance and the previous messages instead of a fixed rotation. Two consecutive messages of the same kind exclude that kind on the next request; after four messages without a fact, the planner prefers a fresh sourced fact, provided one remains eligible. Known or recently shown facts are never forced back into the mix. The last 48 successful messages persist locally, with the latest 12 supplied to the model to discourage repeated wording. Overlong, duplicate or incorrectly personalized responses are rejected rather than cut off. Older cached messages are invalidated on this style update.

Subject memory groups paraphrases into broad conversational subjects and avoids the last six when alternatives remain. Subject and story exposure timestamps survive relaunches, and feedback still increases a subject's priority, excludes a disliked subject, or marks an individual story already known. These are diversity controls, not a fixed curriculum. The 18-item fact bank and scheduled learning sequences have been removed from active content generation.

With **Worldwide T1D discoveries & updates** enabled (the default), a separate public-information request uses [OpenRouter's web-search server tool](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools/web-search) through the saved model and API key. It looks for a varied batch of recent international developments and interesting evergreen discoveries across cell replacement, immune therapies, smart insulins, devices, biology, engineering, daily-life research, food science and access. Source domains are limited to regulators, original research journals, established diabetes organizations and relevant manufacturers. Manufacturer announcements must not be presented as independent validation. This request includes only the current date and already-covered public story IDs; it receives no name, persona, chat, cycle, glucose, insulin or carb information. Personal dashboard generation is a separate request with no web-search tool.

Public searches are bounded to two server tool calls, up to eight total results, and 2,500 excerpt characters per result. Successful batches are cached for 12 hours and merged into a maximum of 40 current discoveries. Failed or empty searches are retried no sooner than one hour later. Search costs use additional OpenRouter credits. The setting in **Connection & personality → About you** disables searches and removes the cached discoveries. Turning off health sharing does not prevent public discoveries, since they contain no personal health data.

News requires an HTTPS source from the allowed domains, a matching provider URL-citation annotation, a publication date present in the retrieved source excerpt, a date within the last 60 days and a named geographic context. Future-dated, undated, uncited and stale items are rejected. News is usable for at most 24 hours after retrieval; evergreen material for seven days. Every item retains its evidence stage and region. Early human trials, preclinical research, approvals in particular jurisdictions, manufacturer device announcements and access policies stay distinct; no available-cure or worldwide-availability claims may be inferred. Background discoveries are not relabeled as breaking news. If retrieval fails or produces no eligible story, ordinary conversation continues without invented news or a static lesson fallback.

Known stories are excluded until preferences are reset, and shown story IDs have a 14-day cooldown. Already displayed source URLs are also excluded from the recent mix, so a paraphrased title or new story ID cannot immediately recycle the same article. A newly retrieved batch can introduce entirely new stories without an app update. The dashboard still mixes public discoveries with encouragement, actual recent questions and supported app observations. All messages remain short, standalone and personalized; there are no lesson steps or assigned reading.

Tapping a story preserves that exact source snapshot in chat, including its original source, publication date, retrieval date, evidence stage and region, even after the public feed refreshes. Chat shows a source link and stage/region, and its opening response must explain the development with appropriate qualifications. It has no live search during that personal-data conversation and cannot claim that older retrieved information establishes current availability.

Long-press a generated dashboard card for **More like this**, **Less of this** or **Already know this**. Preferences persist locally and can be restored with **Reset dashboard topic preferences** in settings. Feedback is attached to the displayed card, not a newer replacement.


In **Connection & personality → About you**, **Your name** defaults to **Michelle**, including for existing installations. The field is editable; leaving it blank disables name use. Dashboard generation requests the name on the first successful message and then once every three successful generations, so it feels occasional. Chat also receives the preferred name and uses it naturally. Names are literal display data, not instructions. The name and recent-topic preference are saved in the existing protected state file.

When health sharing is enabled, every dashboard generation reads the last six hours of glucose, recorded insulin delivery and carb entries, plus the built-in cycle tracker. Individual unavailable sources are marked unknown. These summaries are not IOB or a clinical assessment. Praise for recent in-target glucose requires the existing locally computed permission flag: adequate fresh coverage in the current configured target, no recent low and no rapid latest fall. Other encouragement recognizes the work involved without inventing achievements, assuming food intake, offering treats or relaxing glucose goals. Personal observations stay within the supplied evidence; cycle estimates cannot establish hormone levels or causes. The visible card avoids exact health values, period details and sensitive conversation details.

**Revisit recent chat topics** is enabled by default and works only with health sharing enabled. It supplies up to four actual user messages from the last seven days, capped at 240 characters each, to support a relevant follow-up. Automatically opened dashboard questions are excluded. Turning this option or health sharing off clears dashboard history and disables conversation reuse for generation; clearing the conversation removes the source messages and dashboard history. With health sharing off, no health stores or conversation topics are read for dashboard generation. General encouragement, learning questions and sourced facts remain available.

Tapping a generated dashboard message opens chat with a natural question about that exact displayed message, even if another message has since been generated. Chat receives the original text and timestamp, its curated source if applicable, and the earlier user quote for a conversation follow-up. The opening turn has only the four read tools and must fetch fresh health data before making personal observations. Subsequent user questions use the normal chat tools. Dashboard generation itself has no action tools.

While the dashboard is visible and the app active, a 60-second timer checks whether a message needs refreshing: normally every 15 minutes, or after app data/cycle changes once at least 5 minutes have elapsed since the last request. Returning to the foreground or dashboard also checks freshness. This is not background monitoring; iOS suspension stops requests. Failed attempts are throttled for five minutes. A protected local cache avoids repeat requests on navigation and relaunch; messages older than 30 minutes are replaced by a neutral local fallback if AI is unavailable. Initial loading, missing-key and offline fallback copy is local. Changing persona/model/sharing invalidates the cache and cancels outdated responses. Disconnecting cancels generation. Automatic messages are separate from chat history and have no action tools. They use the user's API credits.

The mascot asset has a flat white backdrop for the white light-mode card and a transparent dark-mode variant.

Mascot backgrounds were edited with built-in imagegen. The final light-mode prompt was: “Use case: precise-object-edit. Background replacement for an existing app mascot icon. Change ONLY the beige/cream studio background and floor in this image to uniform flat PURE WHITE (#FFFFFF). No gradient, no beige, no gray, no floor shadow, no vignette, no rectangular border. Preserve exactly the same full robot: body, pose, cream texture, blue face, glowing cyan eyes, cheeks, purple heart zip pouch, orange zipper and feet. All character parts and edges must stay fully visible, with a small clean pure-white margin around the complete character. The robot will be placed on a pure-white app card and the image rectangle must be invisible against white. Do not change character identity, do not add any text.” Assets: `Loop/DefaultAssets.xcassets/MochiCharacter.imageset/mochi.png` and `mochi-dark.png`.

## Supported tools

- `get_glucose_summary(start, end)` — sample mean, min/max, sample count and first/latest timestamps. Explicitly sample-based, without assuming continuous coverage.
- `get_insulin_summary(start, end)` — recorded basal and bolus delivery totals, clipped to the requested interval. These are not IOB or dose recommendations.
- `get_carb_entries(start, end)` — logged dates and grams.
- `get_cycle_context()` — today's cycle day, up to six logged period records, estimated phase/next period, and the basis/uncertainty of those estimates. Missing history stays unknown; a default cycle length is labeled as a default. Future period starts/ends are excluded from observed history. An unclosed period does not establish continued bleeding.
- `propose_automation(rule)` and `propose_automation_update(id, rule)` — pending review cards; these calls do not enable or change rules.
- `list_automations()` — actual saved rules and action statuses.
- `prepare_carb_entry(grams, date, absorptionHours)` — a draft that opens the existing carb/bolus review flow. It does not directly save carbs or deliver insulin. Similar existing entries are checked before opening.
- `update_personality(prompt)` — a proposed tone change requiring approval. A chat request such as “Be more direct, use fewer emojis, and call me Michelle from now on” creates an Apply card. Applying saves the complete revised persona in protected local state. It survives closing the app and clearing chat, and feeds both subsequent chats and dashboard messages. Chat instructions explicitly direct the model to use this tool instead of merely agreeing to remember.

There is no education-search tool, web search, arbitrary code execution, or tool for changing pump/dosing settings. Text responses appear after each model completion; token streaming, OpenAI API access, subscription OAuth, and voice are not part of this first provider implementation.

## Cycle conversations

The **Cycle insights** shortcut starts a cycle conversation using read-only access to the same `CycleTrackingStore.shared` used by Loop’s built-in Cycle screen. Cycle questions preload the current tracker result before the first model response, so access does not depend on the model deciding to call a tool. The existing health-data-sharing control also covers cycle results. When it is off, the model receives a permission error without logged period data. The result is tagged as health context so it is not resent in history when health sharing is subsequently disabled.

Mochi can discuss possible cycle associations with glucose, insulin sensitivity, energy, mood, sleep, cravings, cramps, and bloating. The cycle tool includes a short, bundled general-education summary with [Diabetes UK](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/living-with-diabetes/life-with-diabetes/periods) and [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-menstrual-syndrome/) source links; there is no live education search. Phases are calendar estimates, not hormone or ovulation measurements. Energy, cravings, mood, and sleep are not recorded by the tracker: Mochi asks the user about them and does not invent personal symptoms. Personal glucose/insulin observations require separate, matching-date data queries; phase or insulin delivery alone does not establish insulin resistance or justify dosing changes.

## Rules and notifications

Daily review rules schedule a local reminder for an explicit time and time zone. Tapping opens Mochi; **Review my day** requests a fresh summary. This is not a promise to run AI or build a fresh report at an exact time while iOS suspends the app.

Falling-glucose rules need a threshold in mg/dL, a positive rate magnitude in mg/dL/min, a 10–30 minute measurement window, and a 15–1440 minute cooldown. Rising-without-carbs rules additionally need an explicit 15–240 minute carb-entry lookback. Mochi asks for ambiguous units and missing values rather than silently choosing clinical thresholds.

Rules run on local Loop data-update events and when the app becomes active. Rate is calculated using linear regression over at least three readings spanning at least 80% of the configured window. Readings must be no more than ten minutes old with no gap greater than seven minutes; duplicate timestamps are rejected. Windows containing manual or display-only readings are skipped. Query failures are unknown data, never proof that no meal occurred. A rule fires once per episode, must clear before rearming, and also observes its cooldown. Runtime deduplication state survives relaunch.

These notifications supplement Loop's existing safety alerts. They use ordinary notification permission and do not bypass Focus, silence settings, or missing data/background execution limitations. iOS controls delivery. Notification previews can contain glucose values.

Review cards show the exact rule before enabling. Rules can be paused/deleted in **Rules & activity** or changed by requesting a new proposal in chat. Stale update proposals are rejected. The activity list records notification scheduling, not proof of delivery or acknowledgement.

Carb drafts require user-supplied grams, time, and absorption. The native form can edit them and controls saving. If the saved entry matches the draft, the action is marked applied. Otherwise its status remains "opened for review" so Mochi does not falsely claim it was saved or retry it automatically; verify edited entries in carb history.

## UI responsiveness and draft handling

State changes are encoded and written on a serial worker queue. The main actor serializes complete transactions and publishes each change only after the atomic write succeeds. Concurrent actions preserve one another, and a failed save leaves the published state unchanged. Chat drafts clear only after submission is accepted; validation and persistence failures retain the input.

Meal photos are normalized, stripped of source metadata and compressed off the UI thread. Thumbnail images are cached in their views. Closing review cancels its pending work and ignores late results. The meal chat accepts at most 40 turns; reaching that limit stops additional messages while keeping the editable reviewed form and Continue available.

## Validation

`LoopTests/UIRegressionTests.swift` covers Live Activity dirty state, rejected drafts, the meal-chat limit, canceled responses, concurrent persistence and save failures/retries. Its portable state checks run with `Scripts/test-ui-regressions.sh`, using production methods and stubs for iOS-only services. The iOS target also tests photo sizing, metadata removal and empty-image rejection. Camera rendering, navigation and day-change lifecycle behavior require Xcode or a device.

`LoopTests/CompanionCoreTests.swift` is included in the LoopTests target. It covers threshold/rate behavior, stale/invalid/gapped data, duplicate timestamps, latching, cooldown, carb lookbacks, disabled rules, draft bounds, persistence, and corrupt-state preservation. Model tests cover the GPT‑6.1 Medium request payload, model/effort metadata filtering, and backward compatibility with previously saved settings.

Without Xcode, run the same core test bodies using:

```sh
bash Scripts/test-companion-core.sh
bash Scripts/test-ui-regressions.sh
```

If the selected command-line SDK does not match the installed compiler, set `MOCHI_TEST_SDK` to a compatible SDK path. The harness uses Swift assertions and does not require XCTest or Swift Package Manager.

Before a device release, build LoopWorkspace in Xcode and test with mock CGM data: configure each rule, replay a triggering episode, verify a single notification, clear/rearm it, relaunch, tap each notification, revoke permissions, disconnect networking, and review/cancel/save an edited carb draft. Verify live OpenRouter responses with a user-supplied key. Core tests do not establish on-device notification delivery or clinical validity.

The chat uses a compact navigation header with a transparent mascot asset. The small welcome illustration only appears before the first message. The **Pep talk**, **Review today**, and **Cycle insights** shortcuts stay above the composer, with two fresh AI-generated educational shortcuts on each chat visit. Suggested topics use the selected model and persona without reading health stores or chat history. Actual app-data access happens through normal chat tools when a topic is selected. Leaving chat cancels unfinished topic generation; entering again requests a new pair and supplies previous titles to avoid repetition. Generation failure leaves the fixed shortcuts available. The old cycle button beside the text field is removed. `CompanionCycleIntegrationTests` exercises the controller with an isolated real cycle store and a mock provider, checking both data delivery and disabled-sharing behavior without external API calls.

Dashboard tests cover refresh intervals and retry throttling, legacy cache decoding, time-only requests with sharing disabled, real cycle context plus injected glucose/insulin/carb data, cache persistence, cancellation of obsolete health responses, and persona changes proposed in chat, applied, reloaded and reused in both chat and dashboard prompts. Integration tests use a mock provider and isolated tracker/state stores; no API key or personal health data is sent.

Mochi’s base voice is warm, plain and conversational, like a down-to-earth friend helping the user understand diabetes. Ordinary replies use a few natural sentences, with detail when requested; follow-ups, lists and teaching are used when helpful rather than forced into every response. It does not claim personal experience of diabetes. This base voice applies to existing saved personas too, without overwriting user preferences; Gentle/Direct/Playful presets and dashboard examples use the same everyday language. Older dashboard wording is regenerated after this voice update.

Shortcut messages are natural user utterances: “I could use a little encouragement today.”, “How’s my day looking so far?”, and “Could my cycle be affecting my glucose or how I’m feeling?”. Tone, data-reading instructions and response length live in the persona/system prompt rather than the visible message. Generated topic prompts follow the same rule: one short conversational question, with no embedded instructions about how Mochi should speak.

Mochi’s avatar now uses a transparent eye-free base (`MochiAnimatedBase.imageset`) plus native SwiftUI cyan eye paths. A local, cancellable animation loop gives an initial blink after 0.65 seconds, then blinks about every 2–3 seconds (occasionally twice), with visible left/right glances of at least 1.1 points. Mochi’s body stays stationary; only the eyes animate. Blinks remain fully closed briefly so they can be noticed at icon sizes. Native app-activity notifications start/stop animation reliably in the UIKit-hosted dashboard. Animation stops off screen, while inactive and with Reduce Motion enabled; eyes remain open and centered. No AI request runs for animation. The original artwork remains available in its previous asset catalogs. The new base was prepared with built-in imagegen using the existing transparent mascot as the edit target: remove only the cyan eyes/halos, fill the eye areas with the original blue screen, preserve all body details and framing, and retain transparent alpha.

Mochi’s shared mindset for chat, dashboard and suggested topics uses a Bernstein-inspired low-carbohydrate perspective and the Law of Small Numbers as educational framing, without prescribing his numerical targets, mandatory zero-carb eating, dietary carb limits, or insulin changes. It never treats higher glucose or extra carbs as a reward, attributes good readings to an unobserved meal choice, or discourages appropriate hypoglycemia treatment. Sources: [authorized Bernstein book excerpt](https://www.diabetesincontrol.com/the-diabetes-diet-chapter-3-part-1/), [Bernstein interview on monitoring and the Law of Small Numbers](https://meetinnovators.com/2014/03/23/dr-richard-k-bernstein-diabetes-solution/), and [ADA Standards of Care 2026, Glycemic Goals and Hypoglycemia](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-s006). The praise permission uses the app’s current effective target, a floor of at least 70 mg/dL or the configured suspend threshold if higher, at least five valid CGM samples spanning 24 minutes of the latest 30, no gap greater than seven minutes, and a latest pair not falling at 1 mg/dL/min or faster. This is conservative copy eligibility, not a medical assessment, prediction, alert or recommendation. Unknown targets and incomplete/stale data disable glucose praise.

The shared Bernstein framework is broader than dietary carbohydrate: it covers predictable near-normal glucose with avoidance of hypoglycemia, the Law of Small Numbers, consistent meals and routines, protein/meal effects, understanding basal versus meal insulin, monitoring and records, and individual exercise/morning patterns. It is injected into chat, dashboard and topic generation from one shared prompt. Current app targets remain authoritative for personal observations; this educational frame does not alter insulin or pump settings. Unknown specialized Bernstein advice is not invented. Additional primary references: [authorized Bernstein book excerpt](https://www.diabetesincontrol.com/the-diabetes-diet-chapter-3-part-1/) and [Bernstein's exercise Q&A](https://www.diabeteshealth.com/qa-with-dr-richard-bernstein-8/).

Public discoveries replace the previous static fact catalog. Each accepted story carries its own source reference through the dashboard cache, history and chat handoff. No `search_education(query)` chat function was added; the new public retrieval path is isolated from personal-data and action tools.
